Still Corner: Hidden Patterns

It's this puzzle game where you're looking for things that are literally right in front of you, but somehow you just... don't see them at first.

Mira’s note: “If you’re expecting loud colors and fast moves… you’re in the wrong place.
Here, you just look. And look again.”

A calm street corner. A wall. A window reflection.
Nothing special—until you tilt the view and a hidden structure begins to appear.

What you do: rotate the scene, shift perspective, find the alignment that reveals the pattern.
Mira’s note:
“I don’t know why… but in Still Corner, everything eventually folds into a design.”

Your guide through Still Corner

Mira’s note:
“I’ve lived here long enough to stop calling it strange.
Now I just call it… honest.”
Mira doesn’t solve puzzles for you.
She notices alongside you.She points out details that feel unimportant—until they suddenly matter.

Who she is:
  • A quiet observer who sketches what she sees
  • Someone who trusts angles more than guesses
  • The kind of person who finds symmetry in broken things

Patterns that live inside ordinary scenes

This game isn’t about collecting items or clearing a board.
It’s about discovering structure.

A scene might look like:
  • scattered tiles
  • random lines
  • messy geometry
  • overlapping shapes
  • reflections and shadows
But at the right angle, it becomes a clean geometric statement.

Rotate. Shift. Align.

Mira’s note:
“Don’t rush. This isn’t a puzzle you beat. It’s one you notice.”

Controls couldn't be simpler, but man, it really messes with your brain - in a good way though.

When chaos suddenly becomes clean

This is what Still Corner's really about. You’ll stare at a scene and feel like there’s nothing there. And then—something aligns.

A faint line appears.
A shape completes itself.
A messy cluster becomes symmetry.

That's the whole point, really. That moment when everything just... clicks. Not speed. Not noise. Just quiet clarity.

From gentle hints to deep structures

  • Early levels:

    • simple patterns

    • strong visual cues

    • wide tolerance

  • Mid levels:

    • multi-layer geometry

    • reflections and overlapping lines

    • partial structures you must “complete” mentally

  • Advanced levels:

    • near-invisible alignments

    • pattern rules that evolve

    • minimal hints

    • tighter precision requirements

Still calm. Still minimal. Just more subtle, more demanding.
